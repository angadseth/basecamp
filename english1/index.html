01

What this course actually asks

Descriptive, not prescriptive

English 1 is taught by Rajesh Kumar, who heads the Department of Humanities and Social Sciences at IIT Madras, and it is taught the way a linguist teaches: English is a system with rules you can state, not a list of dos and don'ts you absorb. That is why week 1 is vowel and consonant sounds rather than essay writing.

If you already speak English comfortably, the risk is not difficulty — it is familiarity. You will recognise every sentence in the lectures and still not be able to answer "how many syllables, and where does the stress fall", or "which part of speech is this word here". Those are the questions on the paper.

The single best predictor of a bad English 1 result is treating the lectures as background noise because the language is familiar. The course tests metalanguage: the words for talking about English, not English itself.

The other half of the course is skills — reading, listening, speaking and writing, one block each. Those weeks reward practice that you cannot do the night before.

In one line

The structure of English, from the phoneme up to the professional email.

04

How it is graded

May 2026 grading document · confirm against your course page

The standard Foundation formula, with no bonus activities:

T = max( 0.6F + 0.3·max(Qz1, Qz2),  0.45F + 0.25Qz1 + 0.3Qz2 )

English has a different eligibility rule

Most Foundation courses let you count Mock Quiz 1 as one of the eight scores in the eligibility average. English 1 and English 2 do not: the grading document asks for the average of the best 5 of the first 7 weekly assignment scores alone, plus attendance at one quiz.

Practically, that means you have seven chances instead of eight, and each weekly assignment matters a little more here than elsewhere.

Weeks 11 and 12 are outside the eligibility window but inside the end term — and in this course they are the writing weeks, which carry very answerable marks.

05

Where the marks actually go

My read, from this course and from what people ask about
01

Week 8, entirely

Aspiration, word stress and syllabification is the most technical content in either English course, it sits after the eligibility window, and it is unlike anything else on the paper. It is also short. Skipping it is the cheapest mistake available.

02

Naming parts of speech in context

The same word changes class depending on the sentence — "book" is a noun in one clause and a verb in the next. Questions test the word in place, not the word in a list.

03

Subject–verb agreement with a long subject

Anything of the shape "one of the…", "the list of…", "each of the…" is designed to make you agree with the nearest noun instead of the head noun. Slow down and find the head.

04

Assuming fluency is preparation

Speaking English well and answering questions about English are different skills. The comfortable students are the ones most likely to walk in under-prepared.
