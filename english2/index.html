01

What this course actually asks

A skills course wearing a grammar course's name

English 2 is taught by Rajesh Kumar, the same instructor as English 1, and the first thing to understand is that it is not a repeat. English 1 was mostly the system — sounds, word classes, tenses. English 2 spends three weeks on clause patterns and then nine weeks on using the language: listening, speaking, reading, writing, and finally the social register that decides whether a correct sentence also lands well.

That last part is the genuinely useful bit, and the bit people dismiss. Weeks 11 and 12 are about how to say something to a stranger, a senior, a team — and those are the sentences you will write for the rest of your career, in every project report and every email to a course team.

The lectures open with figures of speech — similes, metaphors, alliteration, irony — which tells you the register of the course. It is a literature department's view of communication, not a grammar drill.

English 1 is a hard prerequisite. Nothing in the Diploma requires English 2 directly, but it is one of the eight you must finish before any Diploma course opens.

In one line

Clause patterns, then the four skills, then how to sound like someone people want to work with.

03

Weeks 1 to 3, in seven shapes

Every English clause is one of these

Three weeks of "patterns in sentences" comes down to seven arrangements of subject, verb, object, complement and adverbial. Pick one and see it labelled. The exam asks you to identify the pattern, so practise labelling rather than rewriting.

Clause patterns

pick a shape

The test that separates SVA from SV: take the adverbial away. "The meeting is" is not a sentence, so the adverbial is obligatory and the pattern is SVA. "The audience applauded loudly" survives losing loudly, so that one is plain SV with an optional extra.

04

How it is graded

May 2026 grading document · confirm against your course page

The standard Foundation formula, with no bonus activities:

T = max( 0.6F + 0.3·max(Qz1, Qz2),  0.45F + 0.25Qz1 + 0.3Qz2 )

Seven chances, not eight

Like English 1, this course's eligibility rule is the average of the best 5 of the first 7 weekly assignment scores, plus attendance at one quiz. Mock Quiz 1 is not in the list the way it is for the maths, statistics and computing courses.

There is also no bonus activity here, so there is nothing to make up a weak exam with. The three components in the formula are all you have.

Weeks 11 and 12 — the social skills block — sit outside the eligibility window and inside the end term.

05

Where the marks actually go

My read, from this course and from what people ask about
01

Assuming it repeats English 1

It does not. English 1's phonetics and tenses do not reappear; sentence patterns and the four skills are new content with their own vocabulary.

02

Confusing SVC with SVOC

Both end in a complement. In SVC it describes the subject; in SVOC it describes the object. Ask "what does this word describe" before naming the pattern.

03

Treating listening as unexaminable

Two full weeks go to it. It is tested through comprehension questions, and it is the one skill that genuinely cannot be crammed — ten minutes a day from week 1 is the whole strategy.

04

Skipping the social skills weeks

Outside eligibility, inside the end term, and the most quotable content in the course. Register and politeness questions are easy marks for anyone who watched the lectures once.

What to do in week one

Term starts 2 October 2026
  1. Learn the seven patterns firstThey run for three weeks and appear in both quizzes. The widget above is the whole block.
  2. Start listening from day oneOne 6 Minute English episode a day. By week 4 you will be ahead of the course instead of behind it.
  3. Do all seven early assignmentsEnglish 2, like English 1, does not count Mock Quiz 1 toward eligibility.
  4. Record yourself onceNinety seconds explaining something you know. Painful, and the fastest feedback you will get before weeks 6 and 7.
  5. Keep a phrase book, not a word listWhole polite formulations — "would you be able to", "I'd like to check whether" — are what weeks 11 and 12 are actually about.
