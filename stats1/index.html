01

What this course actually asks

Two courses wearing one name

Statistics 1 is taught by Usha Mohan of the Department of Management Studies at IIT Madras, and it is really two courses joined in the middle. Weeks 1 to 4 are descriptive statistics — summarise a dataset, draw it honestly, measure how two variables move together. Weeks 5 to 12 are counting and probability, ending in named distributions.

The first half rewards care. The second half rewards a different skill entirely: being able to set up a problem before solving it. "Three cards are drawn without replacement" is a modelling question dressed as arithmetic, and the arithmetic is never the hard part.

The week-7 cliff is real. People coast through six comfortable weeks, then hit probability and discover they have not built a study habit yet. If you are taking four courses, this is the one to stay ahead in.

It feeds Statistics 2 directly, and nearly everything in the Diploma's machine learning courses assumes it. Expectation and variance, in weeks 10 and 11, are used constantly later.

In one line

Describe data, count possibilities, then reason about uncertainty.

Books

Two volumes are downloadable from the course page — Descriptive Statistics, and Probability and Probability Distributions.

Prescribed: Weiss, Introductory Statistics (10th ed.) and Ross, Introductory Statistics (4th ed.).

02

The twelve weeks

Topics quoted from the official course page
W1

Introduction and type of data. Types of data, descriptive and inferential statistics, scales of measurement.nominal, ordinal, interval, ratio — examinable, and easy to lose marks on

W2

Describing categorical data. Frequency distributions, best practices for graphing categorical data, mode and median for a categorical variable.

W3

Describing numerical data. Frequency tables, mean, median and mode, quartiles and percentiles, range, variance, standard deviation, IQR, five-number summary.the densest week of the first half — see the box below

W4

Association between two variables. Contingency tables and relative frequencies; scatterplot, covariance, Pearson correlation, point bi-serial correlation.

W5

Counting. Addition rule, multiplication rule, factorials.

W6

Permutations and combinations.

W7

Probability. Basic definitions, events, properties of probability.last week that counts toward end-term eligibility — and the start of the hard half

W8

Conditional probability. Multiplication rule, independence, law of total probability, Bayes' theorem.

W9

Random variables. Random experiment, sample space, discrete and continuous random variables, probability mass function, cumulative distribution function.

W10

Expectation and variance of a discrete random variable.

W11

Binomial and Poisson. Bernoulli trials, i.i.d. random variables, binomial random variable and its expectation and variance, Poisson distribution.

W12

Continuous random variables. Area under the curve, properties of a pdf, uniform distribution, exponential distribution.

Two conventions that cost marks

Which standard deviation? Dividing by n gives the population value; dividing by n − 1 gives the sample value. Both are shown above. A question that says "these are all the observations" wants n; one that says "a sample of" wants n − 1.

Which quartiles? This tool uses the median-of-halves rule, excluding the overall median when the count is odd — the convention in the prescribed Weiss text. Other books split differently and get different numbers. Check which one your course uses before an exam.

The five bonus marks

Statistics 1 runs four optional "extra activities" across the term. They are worth 5 marks in total — 3.75 for the weekly activity itself and 1.25 at the instructors' discretion for quality.

  • They are added only if you pass the course.
  • Each activity is peer reviewed. If you do not complete at least five peer reviews, you get zero for that activity — even if you submitted it.

Five marks is the difference between a B and an A at the boundary. Doing the activity and then skipping the reviews is the most avoidable loss in the whole Foundation level.

The eligibility gate

Average of the best 5 of 8 — the first seven weekly assignments plus Mock Quiz 1 — must be 40/100 or more, and you must attend at least one quiz.

05

Where the marks actually go

My read, from this course and from what people ask about
01

Submitting an activity and skipping the reviews

Fewer than five peer reviews means zero for that activity. It is written in the grading document and it still happens every term.

02

Permutation where a combination was wanted

Week 6 lives or dies on one question: does the order matter? Decide that out loud before you write a single factorial, every time.

03

Conditioning the wrong way round

P(A|B) and P(B|A) are different numbers, and week 8's word problems are written so that the natural reading gives you the wrong one. Write the events down as symbols before computing.

04

Confusing correlation's scale

Covariance has units and no bound; Pearson correlation is unitless and lives in [−1, 1]. Week 4 questions check that you know which one changes when you change the units of measurement.
