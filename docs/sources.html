Where every fact came from

Checked 23 September 2026

Nothing on this site is meant to be taken on trust. Everything below is either an official document, something I read myself and say so, or clearly marked as opinion. Where the official record and this site ever disagree, the official record wins — tell me and I will fix the page.

The most important caveat on this site

As of 23 September 2026, the September 2026 grading document had not been published. Every formula, eligibility rule and bonus structure on Basecamp is taken from the May 2026 document. Terms do change rules — GAA going to zero is proof of that. Read your course's grading page in week 1 and trust it over this site.

Not from an official page

Where the site is giving you a view, not a record
ClaimWhat it actually is
Grade bands S ≥ 90, A ≥ 80, B ≥ 70, C ≥ 60, D ≥ 50, E ≥ 40 (used by the hub calculator) The programme's usual scale, not taken from the grading document. The calculator says so on the page. Confirm against your course.
Every "Where the marks actually go" section My own reading of each course and of what people repeatedly get stuck on. Opinion, labelled as such on each page.
"What to do in week one" plans Advice, not rules. The only hard items in them — the SCT, the assignment deadlines, the peer reviews — are sourced above.
Quartile convention in the Statistics 1 widget (median of halves, excluding the median when n is odd) The convention in the prescribed Weiss text. Other books differ, and the widget says so.
Groupings like "weeks 1–6 are algebra, 7–9 calculus, 10–11 graph theory" My grouping of the official week list, to make the shape of a course visible.
Maths 2's week count The course page says "eleven weeks of coursework" and lists eleven; the grading document still uses the standard "W11/W12" wording. The site flags the mismatch rather than resolving it.

Left out on purpose

Things that exist and are not linked here
  • Graded-assignment and quiz answer sites. Several exist and are easy to find. They are not linked from any page on this site, and they never will be.
  • Anything that would help you break the LLM rule on Python's programming assignments.
  • Paid coaching and "solutions" services.
  • Discourse threads. They are login-only, so they cannot be checked by a reader and are not cited.

Every external link on this site was opened and checked on 23 September 2026. If one has rotted since, it is a bug — the site would rather drop a link than keep a dead one.
