01

What this course actually asks

Ordinary Python, extraordinary admin

Programming in Python is taught by Satyadev Nandakumar of the computer science department at IIT Kanpur, and the content is a standard first Python course: conditionals, loops, collections, files, modules, and a final week of pandas and numpy. If you have programmed before, none of it will surprise you.

The course is still the one people most often have to repeat, and the reason is structural. It has two proctored programming exams, no Quiz 2, and four different eligibility rules that use different weeks. Miss any one of them and the exams you did write stop counting.

Using an LLM on a programming assignment is plagiarism

The grading document states it directly: "In each programming assignment, be it any course or any OPPE, taking help from LLMs (e.g. ChatGPT, Gemini) partially or completely is considered plagiarism", and "unless explicitly permitted, do not use LLMs. Using LLMs is considered a violation of honour code."

You may discuss and learn from each other; the assignment itself is expected to be your own. This page will not help you around that, and it is not a rule worth testing.

03

Am I eligible?

Four gates, four different sets of weeks

Put in your eight weekly programming-assignment averages — A1 to A8 — and every gate is checked at once. Note what makes this course different: for the OPPEs there is no "best of". Each of the four relevant weeks must reach 40 on its own.

Eligibility checker

nothing is stored or sent

The SCT is not optional. The grading document says it in capitals: OPPE 1 and OPPE 2 will not be scheduled for students who fail to complete the System Compatibility Test. It also gates the bonus marks — attending mock tests earns you nothing unless the SCT is done.

04

How it is graded

May 2026 grading document · confirm against your course page

The only Foundation course that does not use the standard formula:

T = 0.15·Qz1 + 0.4·F + 0.25·max(PE1, PE2) + 0.2·min(PE1, PE2)

There is no Quiz 2. PE1 and PE2 are the two programming exams, and between them they are worth 45% of the course — more than the end term. The better of the two is weighted higher, so a weak first OPPE is recoverable.

The rule that overrides the formula

To get a grade at all you must attend the end term and score 40 or more in at least one OPPE. If you miss that, your total score is irrelevant:

05

Where the marks actually go

My read, from this course and from what people ask about
01

One weak assignment week

Everywhere else in Foundation a bad week is absorbed by a "best 5 of" rule. Here, A1 through A8 are checked individually at 40. A single week below the line removes an entire OPPE.

02

Leaving the SCT until later

It is a system check, it takes minutes, and without it neither programming exam is scheduled. Do it the week it opens.

03

Output that is nearly right

Programming exams are auto-graded on exact output. A stray space, a missing newline, or printing 418.0 where 418 was wanted scores zero for a correct program. Read the output spec twice.

04

Practising by reading

Twenty-three hours of video is the shortest lecture set in Foundation, which tells you where the work is meant to happen. The OPPE is typed under time pressure; the only preparation is typing under time pressure.

07

What to do in week one

Term starts 2 October 2026
  1. Do the SCT the day it opensNo SCT means no OPPE means no grade. It is the single highest-value ten minutes in the entire Foundation level.
  2. Treat every weekly assignment as a gateA1 to A8 are checked individually at 40. There is no dropping your worst week in this course.
  3. Read the output spec, then read it againAuto-grading is exact. Practise printing precisely what is asked, with no extra text.
  4. Type, do not watchTwenty-three hours of lectures is the shortest set in Foundation. The rest of the time is meant to be spent writing code.
  5. Do the mock tests — after the SCTAveraging 40 or more across the mocks before a quiz is worth 2 bonus marks, but only if the SCT is done.
